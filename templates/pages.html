{% if page=='about' %} ABOUT THE BRIEF

A little more understanding. A little less overload.

The Brief brings together AI news from the AI Empowerment Observatory and explains what each source says in everyday language. You can read a short summary, follow the original reporting and join a thoughtful conversation.

Headlines that tell you something

Our own headlines lead with the main development or finding. A company's claim stays a claim. A forecast stays a forecast. A small study stays a small study. If we have not written a summary, we show the publisher headline and link directly to the reporting.

Automated tools draft new summaries from the retained source text. Checks look for unsupported claims, changed sample groups, copied wording and misleading certainty. These checks can make mistakes; the original reporting remains the reference.

What changes for people and for AI?

We read those questions separately. A story can describe benefits, downsides, both, or no direction on either side. Incomplete evidence remains visible without being treated as a finding.

Five discovery markets

The Observatory gathers English, French and Chinese reporting through searches in Canada, China, France, the United Kingdom and the United States. These are discovery markets, not a claim that the story happened there or that the sample represents the whole world. A story discovered in more than one market can appear in more than one filter.

Research with its limits attached

The research feed links to papers and uses their actual findings. Preprints are labelled. When a summary is based on an abstract, we say so. Publication venue, study scope and limitations stay alongside the finding.

A new reading order each day

The Observatory supplies a weekly news edition. Each day, we rotate its front-page highlights and reading order across the five discovery markets. Every story remains available; its reporting date stays the same. You can choose newest reporting or actual readership instead.

A daily pause

Art, poetry, reading, a quotation and music offer another way to spend a moment here. Five works are selected daily from a stored collection of 2,000 entries: 400 artworks, 400 poems, 400 short readings, 400 quotations and 400 music recordings. Entries do not repeat within the 400-day calendar. Creator nationality or cultural background, translation credits, original dates and sources stay with each work. Historical views belong to their authors; the selection is an invitation to explore. Images and passages are stored in advance. Music loads from its credited host when you press Play. The date of our selection does not make an older work new. Creative works are separate from the news and research counts.

Conversations and readership

Reading is open. An email sign-in lets you like and discuss a story. Comments are checked before publication. The most-read list uses opted-in sessions with at least 20 seconds of active reading and half of the article reached, once per story per session per day. It is an indication of readership, not a measure of quality or a verified count of people.

Independent editorial choices

Advertising does not change story ordering, classification or the research record. Adverts do not buy favourable summaries.

A research initiative

The Brief is part of Kedma Hamelberg's work on AI, communication and human empowerment. Visit the AI Empowerment Observatory →

{% elif page=='privacy' %} PRIVACY

Stay informed on your terms.

You can read and share stories without an account. Device-local saves and your privacy choice use browser storage. Signing in stores the session needed to keep your account available.

News notifications

Browser alerts are optional and separate from analytics. When you enable them, we store a browser delivery address, encryption keys and a private token used to manage your choice. Your browser provider handles delivery. We do not need your name or email. The token stays on your device; the server stores only its hash. You can turn alerts off on the news updates page or in browser settings. Expired subscriptions are removed. Unused subscriptions expire after 180 days, and delivery records are retained for 30 days.

Optional analytics

Analytics starts only after you choose to allow it. It can record story IDs, reading time, page use, sharing-button choices and outbound-link clicks. We do not send your email or comment text to Google Analytics. A share-button click tells us you opened a sharing option, not that you published a post.

Your account and comments

Your account email is private. Your chosen display name and approved comments are public. You can delete your comments, remove likes and saves, or delete your Brief community profile from the account page. Sign-in authentication records may also be used by other services sharing this account; contact the project about removing them.

Retention

Detailed opted-in reading events are deleted after 90 days. Aggregate readership can remain. Comments, likes and account saves remain until you remove them or the service removes them. Public comments can be copied by other readers, even after deletion here.

Advertising

When Google advertising is active, ads are placed automatically. Google's consent message controls advertising choices. You can revisit those choices from Privacy choices.

Music and external sources

Music is loaded from its original host only after you press play. That host receives the network request needed to deliver audio. Opening a source link takes you to the source’s own website and privacy terms.

Contact

For privacy questions or a data request, use the contact details on the Observatory privacy page.
